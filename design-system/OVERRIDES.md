# Design direction overrides

`design-system/precedent/MASTER.md` is generated by the ui-ux-pro-max skill and is
the source of truth for Precedent. This file records the places where the build
brief's design constraints **deliberately override** the generator, and why. It is
the only sanctioned place where a deviation may be recorded — undocumented
divergence from MASTER is a bug.

Retrieval rule is unchanged: when implementing page X, read
`design-system/precedent/pages/x.md` first; if it exists its rules override
MASTER; otherwise MASTER applies. This file sits above both.

## How MASTER was generated

The first generation run returned *Vibrant & Block-based* — "bold, energetic,
playful", key effects of animated patterns and 32px+ display type, and an
explicit anti-pattern of "text-heavy pages". That is the opposite of a reference
tool whose entire payload is dense structured text.

Rather than hand-edit the generated file, MASTER was regenerated with the skill's
own dials set to the intended direction:

```
--variance 1   # centered / minimal, not bold / asymmetric
--density 8    # dense, dashboard-like — results are scanned in bulk
--motion 2     # subtle
```

That produced the palette and typography now in MASTER: Inter, a navy/slate
neutral base, mood "minimal, clean, swiss, functional, neutral, professional".
Those values are used as-is. The overrides below are what remains.

## Overrides

### 1. Style family

MASTER says **Exaggerated Minimalism** — "oversized typography, font-weight 900,
`clamp(3rem, 10vw, 12rem)`, massive whitespace".

**Override:** Swiss Modernism / Minimalism with an Editorial Grid influence on
profile pages, and Bento Box Grid confined to the comparison view. Type scale tops
out at 48px (`--text-4xl`) and is used once per page at most.

**Why:** oversized display type is a landing-page device. It costs vertical space
on exactly the surfaces — results lists, timelines — that need to show many
comparable records at once. The brief calls for desktop density and progressive
disclosure on mobile.

### 2. Page pattern and CTA

MASTER's page pattern is *Marketplace / Directory*, with a section order ending in
"CTA (Become a host/seller)" and a navbar CTA of "List your item".

**Override:** the marketplace section order does not apply to any in-product
surface. There is no featured-listings carousel and no trust/safety marketing
section inside the app. The search bar being the primary CTA is kept — that part
is correct for this product.

**Why:** these are conversion-funnel patterns. Precedent's product surfaces are a
reference tool; the marketing page may be warmer, the app must be sober. Product
principle 4 also rules out engagement mechanics.

### 3. Two type families, not one

MASTER pairs Inter with Inter.

**Override:** Inter (`--font-ui`) for UI chrome, data labels, and all numerics;
**Source Serif 4** (`--font-text`) for pathway prose — step descriptions, obstacle
and resolution text, reflections.

**Why:** the brief requires one high-legibility text face for pathway content and
one geometric/grotesque for chrome. The split also does real work: it makes
author-written evidence visually distinct from platform-generated structure, so a
reader can tell at a glance what Precedent is asserting versus what a submitter
claimed. Tabular numerals (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) are mandatory on
dates, durations, GPA bands, and compensation bands.

### 4. Saturated colour is reserved for encoding

MASTER offers a general accent/CTA green (`#059669`) alongside the navy primary.

**Override:** saturated colour appears in only two places — verification tiers and
step-type coding. UI chrome, including primary buttons, uses brand navy. If a
colour is not encoding data, it is not saturated.

**Why:** on a results page showing 40 pathways, every saturated pixel competes
with the two channels that actually carry meaning. Reserving colour is what makes
the timeline readable at a glance.

### 5. Step-type palette: 7 hue families, not 14 hues

There are 14 step types. A categorical palette may not exceed 8 hues and hues are
never cycled, so step types are grouped into **7 hue families** (education, work,
output, movement, process, money, turning point) with the icon and visible label
carrying the within-family distinction.

`setback` is given **no hue at all**. It renders recessed — sunken surface, muted
ink, neutral rule.

**Why:** a 14-hue palette is not distinguishable by anyone, and is actively
hostile under colour vision deficiency. Leaving `setback` neutral serves the brief
twice: it keeps setbacks "recessed, not hidden", and it keeps red reserved for
destructive actions so a documented setback never reads as an error state.

**Validation:** the 7 hues were checked with the data-viz palette validator
against Precedent's own surfaces, not the validator's defaults.

| Mode | Surface | Result |
|---|---|---|
| light | `#FFFFFF` | lightness band, chroma floor, CVD separation (worst adjacent ΔE 9.1), normal-vision floor (worst 19.6) all **PASS**; contrast **WARN** on aqua/yellow/magenta |
| dark | `#131A23` | all six checks **PASS**, worst adjacent CVD ΔE 8.4, all hues ≥ 3:1 |

The light-mode contrast WARN triggers the relief rule, which is satisfied
structurally: every timeline node renders a visible text label and type name, and
the step-type legend is always present. Colour never carries step identity alone.

Re-run after any palette change:

```bash
node scripts/validate_palette.js "#2A78D6,#EB6834,#1BAF7A,#EDA100,#E87BA4,#008300,#4A3AA7" --mode light --surface "#FFFFFF"
```

### 6. Depth via hairlines, not shadows

MASTER specifies a four-level shadow scale with `translateY(-2px)` card hovers.

**Override:** separation is carried by 1px hairline borders. Shadows are limited to
genuinely floating layers (popovers, modals, mobile sheets). Card hover is a
background wash, not a lift.

**Why:** MASTER's own anti-pattern list forbids layout-shifting hovers, and a
results grid of lifting cards is visual noise in a tool built for bulk scanning.

### 7. Motion

MASTER's motion module suggests GSAP ScrollTrigger scroll reveals.

**Override:** no scroll-reveal animation anywhere. Motion is limited to state
transitions — timeline node expansion, filter application, save confirmation — at
150–300ms. No GSAP dependency; CSS transitions and platform-native animation only.

**Why:** content that fades in on scroll is hostile to a reference tool users
scan, and to crawlers. Product principle 4 rules out attention mechanics.

### 8. Dark mode is selected, not derived

Dark values are chosen per role and validated against the dark surface. They are
never an automatic inversion of the light set. Dark declarations appear under both
`prefers-color-scheme: dark` and `[data-theme='dark']` so an explicit user toggle
wins in both directions.

### 9. The sequence ribbon is a third surface for the step encoding

Override #4 fixes the two channels allowed to carry saturated colour. The
**sequence ribbon** (`apps/web/components/sequence-ribbon.tsx`) is a new surface
for the *second* of those channels, not a third channel: one segment per step, in
order, on the existing step-family hues, shown on result cards, the landing-page
index rows, and the profile header.

It replaces the earlier treatment of seven 6px dots.

**Why:** the shape of a route — how long it ran, what kind of work it was made
of, and where it stalled — is the thing a reader scans for, and a dot row was too
small to carry any of it. At ribbon scale the encoding becomes readable
pre-click: a route that is mostly coursework reads blue, and a stall reads as a
grey notch in the run.

Two constraints keep this inside override #4 rather than widening it:

- The ribbon introduces **no new hues**. It draws only from the seven step
  families already defined in `packages/ui-tokens/src/step-types.ts`.
- It always appears with `SequenceLegend`. Colour that a reader cannot decode is
  decoration, and decoration is exactly what override #4 exists to prevent.

`setback` keeps its recessed treatment from override #5 — the neutral rule colour
at reduced height, so a stall reads as an interruption rather than an error.

### 10. Compare packs steps into lanes

The comparison view gave every step its own row, which made each pathway a
staircase as tall as its step count and pushed the second route below the fold.

**Override:** steps are greedily packed into the fewest non-overlapping lanes, so
a sequential route collapses to a single lane and a second lane appears only
where two steps genuinely ran at once. Year gridlines run behind the bars.

**Why:** a comparison you have to scroll between is not a comparison. Packing
puts two routes on one screen while still showing overlap where it exists —
which is precisely when the reader needs to see it.

### 11. The landing page is a marketing surface, and is styled as one

Overrides #1 and #4 keep the type scale capped at 48px and the palette neutral.
Both were written about the **tool** — results lists, timelines, profiles — and
#1 says so explicitly: *"oversized display type is a landing-page device. It
costs vertical space on exactly the surfaces — results lists, timelines — that
need to show many rows."*

The landing page is not one of those surfaces. It shows one message and six
cards, and its primary reader is a sixteen-year-old deciding in about four
seconds whether this product is for them. Styled like the tool, it read as a
database console and answered that question wrong.

**Override, scoped to `apps/web/app/page.tsx` only:**

| | Tool | Landing |
|---|---|---|
| Display scale | caps at `4xl` (48px) | `5xl` / `6xl` (60/72px) |
| Headline face | Inter | Nunito (`fontFamily.display`) |
| Page surface | slate `pageBg` | warm `landingBg` |
| Primary action | 36px rounded-md | 48px pill |

**Why these four and nothing else:**

- **Scale and roundness** carry almost all of the difference in how approachable
  a page feels. Inter at 72px is competent and cold.
- **Warmth** is the cheapest signal that a page was made for a person rather than
  for a query. The slate ramp is correct behind data and unwelcoming in front
  of it.
- The **pill** reads as a button to someone who has never used the product.

**What this override does NOT license:**

- No new hue. The landing uses brand navy and the existing step-family palette;
  override #4 is untouched, and there is still no third saturated channel.
- Nothing here crosses into the tool. `font-display`, the `5xl`/`6xl` sizes, and
  the `landing-*` surfaces appear on the landing page and nowhere else. A results
  row set in Nunito would be a bug.
- No claims invented for effect. The social-proof line is generated from
  `getStats()`, and the six cards are real records from the index — the landing
  page renders the same live components as the tool, so it cannot drift from
  what the product actually is.

The warm surfaces are held to the same contrast gate as every product surface —
`landingBg`, `landingSurface`, and `landingSunken` are asserted at ≥4.5:1 against
body ink in both themes in `packages/ui-tokens/test/contrast.test.js`.

## Retained from MASTER without change

- Inter as the UI/chrome family, and the navy `#1E3A5F` primary
- The neutral slate base (`#F8FAFC` page, `#0F172A` ink, `#E2E8F0` border)
- The 4px spacing grid and 150–300ms transition range
- The entire pre-delivery checklist, and the forbidden-patterns list (no emoji as
  icons, `cursor-pointer` on clickables, visible focus states, 4.5:1 minimum
  contrast, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, no horizontal scroll on mobile)
- "Search bar is the CTA. Reduce friction to search."
- The search page's high-density directive and 12-column grid
- The search page's explicit avoidance of a blank "0 results" screen — implemented
  as the adjacent-pathways empty state
